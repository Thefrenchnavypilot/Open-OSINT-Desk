# OSINT methodology

## A repeatable workflow

1. **Define the question.** State exactly what is being checked, the relevant time window and geographic scope.
2. **Discover.** Find primary documents, official statements, datasets and independent reporting.
3. **Preserve.** Record the URL, publisher, publication date, access date and relevant excerpt or archived reference where lawful.
4. **Verify.** Check origin, date, location, context and whether the material is original or recycled.
5. **Corroborate.** Seek independent sources; multiple outlets repeating one wire report are not necessarily independent.
6. **Analyze.** Separate observations from interpretations and consider alternative explanations.
7. **Assess confidence.** Explain evidence quality, gaps, contradictions and what would change the assessment.
8. **Report.** Present a concise, traceable account with links and explicit uncertainty.
9. **Review harm.** Remove unnecessary personal data and avoid details that could enable targeting or harassment.

## Evidence labels

- **CONFIRMED FACT:** supported by strong, traceable evidence.
- **ATTRIBUTED STATEMENT:** a named source or institution said it; this does not automatically prove the statement.
- **ASSESSMENT:** reasoned interpretation based on available evidence.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** plausible explanation that remains unconfirmed.
- **UNVERIFIED:** insufficient evidence to establish the claim.

## Source assessment checklist

- Is the source identifiable and accountable?
- Is it close to the original event or document?
- Does it provide evidence, not merely assertion?
- Is the publication date distinct from the event date?
- Can the information be independently corroborated?
- Are there incentives, biases or limitations?
- Has the material appeared previously in a different context?

## Geolocation and image verification

Use multiple independent visual clues (landmarks, terrain, road layout, signage, shadows, weather and known imagery). Do not rely on one matching feature. Treat metadata as a clue, not proof. Record competing matches and confidence. Do not geolocate private homes or private individuals.

## Confidence language

- **High:** several strong, independent lines of evidence; no major unresolved contradiction.
- **Moderate:** credible evidence exists, but important gaps or dependencies remain.
- **Low:** evidence is limited, indirect, conflicting or difficult to authenticate.

Confidence applies to a specific claim, not to an entire report.
